# Who owns it, and why that matters

## The short answer

**ThinManager is owned by Rockwell Automation** (NYSE: ROK), the Milwaukee-based
industrial automation company — the people behind Allen-Bradley PLCs and the
FactoryTalk software line.

It was not built by Rockwell. It came in by acquisition.

## The history

**Automation Control Products (ACP)** was founded in **1999**, based in Alpharetta,
Georgia. The company's origin story is straightforwardly practical: they saw that
plant-floor PCs were a maintenance liability — every one a box with a fan, a hard
drive and an operating system to patch — and that thin clients removed most of
those failure points. ThinManager was built to manage them.

By the time of acquisition ACP was still small — **fewer than 30 employees** — but
with real reach: installations in roughly **3,000 locations** across **30
countries**, **1,000+ customers**, including close to **30 of the world's top 100
manufacturers**. A small company with a deeply entrenched niche product.

**Rockwell Automation acquired ACP in September 2016** (the press release ran
12 September 2016; some coverage dates the close a few days later). ACP's two
core products at the time were **ThinManager** and **Relevance**.

Rockwell's stated rationale was its **"Connected Enterprise"** strategy —
connecting plant-floor information to the wider enterprise, with mobility and
remote visualization as a component. ThinManager gave them a mature centralized
visualization and endpoint-management layer without building one.

## What the ownership means for you, commercially

This is the part that actually affects a sales call.

**1. This is industrial software, sold by an industrial automation vendor.**
ThinManager now sits inside the **FactoryTalk** product family. Its native
customer is a manufacturing plant with PLCs, HMIs and SCADA. That shapes the
pricing model, the reseller channel, the reference customers, and the vocabulary
the rep will use. A commercial office campus is not the centre of that target
market — which does not make it a bad fit, but does mean you should ask for
references that look like you rather than like a bottling plant.

**2. You are likely buying through a channel, not from Rockwell directly.**
Rockwell sells heavily through distributors and system integrators. The person on
the call may be a reseller or SI rather than a Rockwell employee. Worth
establishing early — it changes who is accountable for the implementation and
who is quoting the services around the licences.

**3. Acquired niche products have a lifecycle risk worth naming.** ThinManager
predates its owner's interest in it by seventeen years and serves a small
market. That is not a red flag on its own — Rockwell has continued releasing
major versions (v14 is the current generation) and the product remains actively
developed. But "what does the roadmap look like for the next five years, and is
this a strategic product or a maintained one?" is a fair and revealing question.

**4. Rockwell is an enterprise vendor with enterprise pricing behaviour.**
Expect volume discounting, multi-year subscription options, and a maintenance
percentage stacked on top of licence cost. See `03-how-it-works.md` for the
licensing mechanics.
